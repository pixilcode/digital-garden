**Link: [The Art of Craftmanship (Monozukuri) in the Age of AI](https://rapha.land/the-art-of-craftsmanship-monozukuri-in-the-age-of-ai/)**

Often in today's world, AI is used to replace the worker. This is not how AI should be used. Instead, AI should be a craftsman's tool. It can be used to speed up the tedious or repetitive parts of coding without replacing the _thinking_ part.

_Monozukuri_ is a Japanese word that "embodies the spirit, skill, and pride of a craftsman". It is a focus on the process, rather than just the outcome. AI is all about the outcome, and therefore can never understand or embody the outcome.

## Related

[[Link - The Value of Things|The Value of Things]] talks about how there is a difference between _utility_ and _meaning_. _Meaning_ is about the _process_, while _utility_ is about the outcome. _Monozukuri_ produces _meaning_, _AI_ produces _utility_.

[[The fall of the theorem economy]] talks about how AI can produce mathematical proofs without producing any understanding of those proofs. Part of the value of proofs is the understanding that comes with discovering a proof, so this renders AI proofs somewhat worthless. In other words, without _monozukuri_, mathematical proofs don't have as much meaning.
