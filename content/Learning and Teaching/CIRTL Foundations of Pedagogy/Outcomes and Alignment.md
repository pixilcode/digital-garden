I'm thinking of two classes: my systems programming class, taught by Dr. Deccio; and my cryptography class, taught by Dr. Pratt. One thing that I remember both of these teachers doing is **remembering my name**. This helped me to feel more comfortable talking to them and interacting with them.

Dr. Deccio also did a good job of **reviewing the content of the previous class** and **giving time for questions**. This helped me to remember more deeply what I learned in that class.

Dr. Pratt made one of our first assignments to **visit his office**. This helped me to know where his office was, and it made me comfortable going there when I needed help with homework.

## Reflect

> [!question] Prompt
> 
> - In your own words, what is the value and benefits of taking the extra time to align different aspects of your course? In what ways do these processes support student learning?
>  
> - In what ways might you continue to refine your approaches to instructional design using the frameworks we have discussed thus far? To the best of your ability, describe a specific assignment, lesson, or module you are excited to refine using these ideas.
>  
> - What shortcomings or underemphasized elements have you noticed within the taxonomies that we explored? How might your future practice and/or scholarship aim to address these limitations?

It seems like the value in aligning the objectives, assessments, and instruction helps your students to remember what they learn in your course. When everything is aligned, this ensures that everything leads to the same goal, rather than jumping around the content. Each piece of the course contributes to the greater whole.

The value of Bloom's taxonomy is that it helps me to ensure that I'm covering all the levels of understanding. If all my learning activities are in the _Remember_ level, then the students aren't going to get very much out of the class. On the other hand, if too many activities are in the _Evaluate_ or _Create_ levels, then the students won't have a good foundation and will feel frustrated and overwhelmed.

The value of Fink's taxonomy is that it helps me remember that there are different areas that I need to focus on. It can be easy to get stuck focusing solely on _Foundational Knowledge_ and _Application_. However, _Integration_ is where the knowledge really comes into play. In addition, there are areas that aren't directly related to the topic at hand (_Human Dimension_, _Caring_, and _Learning How To Learn_) that are important skills to teach as well. In fact, the most widely applicable skills that a student can learn will come from those areas. So it's important to give those areas their time as well.
